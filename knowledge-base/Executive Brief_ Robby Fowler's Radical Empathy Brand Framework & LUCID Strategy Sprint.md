## **II. The Radical Empathy Brand Framework (7-Part System)**

### **Visual Foundation: The Bridge Metaphor**

**The Setup:**

* **Customer cliff**: Person with problem they cannot solve \+ possibility they cannot secure  
* **Business cliff**: You have the solution \+ can help them secure the possibility  
* **The Gap**: Feels insurmountable to customer (this gap IS the business opportunity)  
* **The Bridge**: Supported by three pillars; all must work together

### **The Seven Elements (In Order)**

#### **1\. Customer**

* **Problem**: Present/past pain they cannot solve alone  
* **Possibility**: Future-oriented goal they cannot secure alone  
* *Key insight*: The gap must feel insurmountable or you don't have a business  
* *Framework function*: First thing to understand; everything else flows from here

#### **2\. Your Business**

* You have the **solution** to their problem  
* You can help them **secure** the possibility  
* The "match made in heaven" alignment

#### **3\. Marketing (First Bridge Pillar)**

* **Job**: Awareness — "going wide"  
* **Nature**: Transactional  
* **Function**: Amplification of core message (microphone \+ speaker analogy)  
* *Key insight*: Marketing without core message \= staring at blank screen wondering what to post  
* *Problem state*: "Spaghetti at wall" marketing \= no core message to amplify

#### **4\. Core Message (Middle Bridge Pillar) — THE MOST FORGOTTEN PILLAR**

* **Job**: Conviction — what marketing amplifies  
* **Function**: What you fundamentally want to communicate  
* **Timing**: Split second after marketing gets attention  
* *Key insight*: This is what most businesses lack entirely  
* *Diagnostic test*: Can all leadership give the same answer in separate rooms?

#### **5\. Branding (Third Bridge Pillar)**

* **Job**: Fondness — "going deep"  
* **Nature**: Relational  
* **Function**: Manifestation (not amplification) of core message  
* **Reality**: "Where rubber meets the road" — truth vs. marketing speak  
* **Ownership**: Customer controls their perception; business influences it  
* **Components**: 6 spokes — Visual (only 10-15%), Verbal, Value, Community, Experiential, Emotional

#### **6\. Radical Empathy Frame**

* Applied to ALL elements above — "carved on the picture frame"  
* Everything built from customer's perspective, not business owner's preferences  
* *Hardest thing to do*: Truly see the world through their eyes  
* **Examples**:  
  * Using Colorado colors (not Nebraska) if customers are Colorado fans  
  * Translating "full-stack development" into customer language  
* **Theological foundation**: Genesis 2 — God notices human is alone, says "not good" (empathy \= noticing)

#### **7\. Character → Values → Purpose (Foundation Layer)**

* **The foundation most marketing books won't discuss**  
* Character shapes what you're willing/unwilling to do  
* Values drive purpose  
* Purpose shapes positioning  
* *Warning*: Marketing success without character foundation \= potential for damage to real lives  
* **Historical cautionary example**: Nazi Germany 1936 Olympics — excellent marketing, corrupt foundation

### **The Customer Journey Flow (Linear)**

1. Discover you because of **Marketing**  
2. Split second to hear your **Core Message**  
3. Build trust through **Branding** experience  
4. Cross the bridge to your solution

### **The Business Development Flow (Reverse)**

1. **WHO** is the customer? (Radical empathy target)  
2. **WHAT** do we want to say? (Core message)  
3. **HOW** do we manifest it? (Brand incarnates message)  
4. **WHERE** do we amplify? (Marketing broadcasts message)

## **III. The LUCID 2-Day Strategy Sprint**

### **What It Is**

A proprietary 2-day intensive strategy sprint that creates foundational messaging and positioning architecture BEFORE any tactical marketing execution (website, launch, content, etc.).

**Core principle:** You can't market effectively what you haven't defined strategically.

**Investment:** $5,000-7,500 (includes 2-day sprint \+ comprehensive LUCID Report)

### **The LUCID Process**

**Day 1: Customer & Success Mapping**

*Customer Transformation Profiles (2-3 personas):*

* Demographics and psychographics  
* Jobs to Do (essential goals)  
* Pains & Problems  
* Objections/Questions  
* Values & Motives  
* Identity/Transformation (who they want to become)  
* Customer Language (actual phrases they use)  
* Decision Criteria (what matters when choosing)  
* Buying triggers and signals

*24-Month Success Vision:*

* External metrics (leads, revenue, clients, referrals)  
* Internal/Team goals (hiring, structure, culture)  
* Revenue targets  
* Personal/Life goals (time freedom, peace of mind)

**Day 2: Brand & Business Architecture**

*Brand Attributes (6 categories):*

* Culture: How would customers describe you?  
* Customer: How would you describe your ideal customers?  
* Voice: How do you sound to others?  
* Benefits: How do others feel after interacting with you?  
* Value: What tangible impact do you have?  
* X-Factor: What's your unfair advantage/superpower?

*Strategic Outputs:*

* What marketing needs to actually DELIVER (outcomes, not content)  
* Website goals and structure priorities  
* Next 90-day strategic priorities  
* Trigger events and buying signals  
* Belief architecture (what prospects must believe to buy)

### **Key Deliverable: The LUCID Report**

Comprehensive documentation including:

1. Challenges to Growth (current obstacles)  
2. Top Resources (unique advantages)  
3. Success Vision (24-month roadmap)  
4. Customer Transformation Profiles (detailed personas)  
5. Trigger Events and Buying Signals  
6. Decision Criteria  
7. Customer Language  
8. Brand Summary (6 attribute categories)  
9. Website Jobs and Goals  
10. Current Website Analysis  
11. Strategic Next Steps

### **Additional LUCID Frameworks**

**Four Questions Framework:**

* Who do you serve?  
* What do you do for them?  
* Why does it matter?  
* Why should they believe you?

**Onlyness Statement:** A differentiation declaration that articulates what makes the business genuinely unique in their market.

**Core Message Formula:** Problem → Solution → Transformation (outcome-focused, not process-focused)

### **Why LUCID Deliberately Withholds Implementation**

LUCID creates strategic foundation WITHOUT tactical execution plans. This is intentional for three reasons:

**From Client Perspective (Foundation Gap):** LUCID creates the foundational clarity almost all clients were missing—at least in actionable format. They had pieces but no master plan coordinating them.

**From Robby's Perspective (Business Model):** The goal is to move clients from LUCID Strategy Sprint into a longer-term Phase II engagement where the LUCID strategy gets systematically implemented and refined.

**From Client Perspective (Implementation Reality):** Everyone gets excited about what LUCID uncovers and clarifies. Almost all fail on implementation if they attempt to return to "normal life"—the same life that created the fog, chaos, and confusion in the first place. They assume they'll somehow implement the strategy on their own. They rarely do.

*This isn't about withholding value—it's about proper sequencing: strategy before execution, and guided implementation over DIY attempts.*

### **LUCID's Positioning Statement**

Before you invest in a website, launch, or marketing overhaul, I help you figure out what to actually say that will get you hired.

### **Trigger Events (When People Need LUCID)**

* About to invest in website redesign  
* Launching new offer or service  
* Overhauling marketing approach  
* Repositioning or rebranding  
* Building marketing infrastructure from scratch  
* Preparing to scale (systematizing founder knowledge before delegation)

## **V. Core Argument Structure (5 Claims Framework)**

### **The Underlying Syllogism**

**Major Premise:** Sustainable business growth requires an integrated framework built on character, designed through radical empathy for the customer, and coordinated by a clear core message that marketing amplifies and branding manifests.

**Minor Premise:** Most businesses fail because they hire contractors without an architect, start with tactics instead of foundation, and lack the core message that makes everything else work together.

**Conclusion:** Therefore, the Radical Empathy Brand Framework provides the missing "master plan" that makes all marketing efforts effective.

### **The 5 Claims (Mapped to Billy Broas Framework)**

#### **CLAIM 1: Empathy — "We Get You"**

You're drowning in marketing tactics without a master plan connecting the pieces. You're the architect-less contractor trying to build a profitable business while juggling disparate marketing efforts, never shown how it all works together.

*Validated by research*: When asked about marketing, participants immediately resonated with "beach balls underwater" metaphor — everything feels like separate pieces they're struggling to coordinate.

Starting with tactics (logo → website → ads) instead of foundation creates scattered "spaghetti at the wall" marketing. Hiring specialists in silos—without an integrated strategy—wastes money and produces inconsistent results that never build momentum.

* Hire marketing agency (executes template without your belief barriers)  
* DIY website redesign (guess at messaging based on competitors)

Sustainable growth requires the 7-part Radical Empathy Brand Framework: starting with Character → Values → Purpose foundation, then building Customer understanding, Business solution, Core Message (the forgotten pillar), Marketing amplification, and Brand manifestation—all through radical empathy lens.

*Key differentiator*: Robby starts TWO LEVELS DEEPER than competitors. Billy Broas starts at "master plan." StoryBrand starts at message. Most marketing books start at tactics. Robby starts at Character Foundation \+ Customer Understanding through Radical Empathy.

#### **CLAIM 4: Trust — "You Can Trust Me"**

20+ years helping SMBs across industries (B2B, B2C, churches, nonprofits) apply this framework. Unique church-to-business background provides perspective most marketing consultants lack—seeing what marketing books won't discuss: character matters more than tactics.

### **Failure Modes (Important Context)**

**Where LUCID "Fails":** The only real failures occur in **lack of implementation following a LUCID Sprint**, not in the sprint itself. The pattern:

1. Client completes LUCID Sprint  
2. Client gets excited about clarity and strategic direction  
3. Client returns to "normal life"—the same life that created the fog/chaos/confusion  
4. Client assumes they'll implement the strategy on their own  
5. Implementation stalls or never happens

**Root Cause:** Robby acknowledges he didn't do a good enough job converting early LUCID clients into Phase II ongoing engagements. Contributing factor: client confusion about Robby's relationship with his brother's separate marketing agency (which focuses on SEO, ads, and websites). This created ambiguity about who would handle implementation.

**Implication for Core Argument:** This failure pattern actually REINFORCES the framework's value proposition:

* The problem isn't the strategy—it's execution without ongoing guidance  
* "Normal life" without strategic coordination recreates the original chaos  
* Phase II engagement isn't upselling—it's the necessary completion of the work

*Interview angle:* Ask Robby how he now positions Phase II to prevent this implementation gap.
