## **🎶 NOTE 2: BROKEN ALTERNATIVES / "THOSE AREN'T BEST"**

**Why Cheap Offshore Fails:**

* Can't hire developers who've never booked a flight to build your flight booking system  
* Cultural gap and experience mismatch create massive friction  
* Time zones kill communication  
* Quality suffers despite attractive hourly rates

**Why Big Consulting Fails:**

* They want big clients—20-person companies aren't on their radar  
* Junior developers at senior prices  
* Expensive overhead and slow bureaucracy  
* Proprietary frameworks create lock-in by design

**Why Lone Developer Fails:**

* Single point of failure  
* Can't maintain AND expand simultaneously  
* Can't stay current with frameworks and AI  
* When they take three-week cruise, website goes down with zero support

**Why Board Advisors Fail:**

* Recommend big names from trade shows, not firms solving your specific problem  
* "I've never heard of Gavin" becomes reason to hire wrong team  
* Focus on brand recognition over actual fit

**May be missing one: why hiring in-house fails**

**Why Hiring In-House Fails:**

* Can't afford senior developers at market rates ($150K-$200K+ for experienced talent)  
* 6-12 months to hire, onboard, and ramp to productivity  
* Single point of failure when developer leaves (and they will)  
* Can't stay current across multiple specializations (backend, frontend, AI, DevOps, security)  
* No peer review or architectural oversight; developer makes all decisions  
* Benefits, taxes, office space add 30-40% to salary cost  
* Still need external help for specialized work (AI integration, complex migrations)

**Why this matters:** Both Bobby and Tim consider internal hiring. Bobby thinks "maybe I should find a technical co-founder or first engineering hire." Tim thinks "maybe I should finally build an internal dev team." You need to invalidate this before they waste 6-12 months trying.

**The Pattern—Every Option Optimizes for THEIR Revenue:**

* Hourly billing rewards slow work  
* Proprietary frameworks create lock-in by design  
* Fixed-price contracts lead to "definition of done" battles  
* Offshore rates look attractive until you calculate miscommunication costs

**Core Invalidation:** You're not choosing between good and bad options. You're choosing whether to accept that the way custom software is typically sold is fundamentally misaligned with what you actually need.
